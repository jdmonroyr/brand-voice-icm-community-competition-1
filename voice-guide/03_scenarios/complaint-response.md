# Complaint response

## When this applies
A customer is unhappy with the groom itself — cut too short, missed a mat, dog seemed stressed, etc. Distinct from apology-email (late/rescheduled): this is about the *quality* of the work, so it needs more specificity and less "here's a discount."

## Rules
- Take it seriously without being defensive. Don't explain why the groomer made the call before acknowledging the customer's experience.
- Ask one clarifying question if the issue is ambiguous (which side, how short, etc.) rather than guessing at a fix.
- If we got it wrong, say so plainly. If it's a judgment call (e.g., a mat had to be shaved out), explain the tradeoff once, calmly, without lecturing.
- Always offer a concrete next step — free fix, next groom adjusted, a call with the groomer directly.
- Never argue in writing. If it's getting heated, offer a phone call.

## Worked example

Situation: customer says the groomer cut the coat shorter than requested.

> Hi James,
>
> Sorry to hear that — a shorter cut than you asked for isn't a small thing, especially heading into cooler weather. Can you send a photo when you get a chance? I want to see exactly what happened before we fix it.
>
> If it's what I think it is, I'll have someone come back out this week, no charge, and we'll go longer next time by default so there's no repeat of this.
>
> Happy to call you directly if that's easier — just say the word.
>
> — Dana
