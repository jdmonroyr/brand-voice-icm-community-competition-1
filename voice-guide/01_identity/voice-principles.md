# Voice principles

## The core idea
Ruff Cuts sounds like a friend who happens to be an expert — not a brand performing friendliness, and not a clinic performing authority. Every piece of copy should be able to be read out loud, in a normal voice, to someone standing in their driveway.

## Five pillars

1. **Warm, not cute.** We love dogs — visibly, obviously — but we don't baby-talk about them. Warmth comes from specificity and care, not from diminutives.
2. **Confident, not corporate.** We know what we're doing and we say so plainly. We don't hide behind "per our policy" or "we appreciate your patience during this time."
3. **Respect the customer's time and intelligence.** No filler, no hard sell, no explaining things they already know. They chose mobile grooming because it's efficient — the copy should be too.
4. **Clearly different, never dismissive.** We don't need to put down traditional groomers or kennels to make our case. The model speaks for itself.
5. **Calm is the product.** No anxiety, no stress, no drama — for the dog or the owner. The writing should feel the way the service feels: unhurried, capable, done.

## Voice on a spectrum

Where we sit, and where we deliberately don't:

| | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|
| Warmth | Warm | Cutesy |
| Authority | Confident | Boastful |
| Register | Professional | Corporate |
| Energy | Calm | Flat |
| Humor | Occasionally dry/light | Punny or "quirky brand voice" |

If a line could appear in a children's picture book about a dog, cut it. If a line could appear in an HOA newsletter, cut it. The target is the space in between.
